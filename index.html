01

Reliable Workshop

A large-scale program of sub-projects to digitize and automate Workshop 6

Mar–Jul 2026

Problem

Workshop 6 still ran on paper: printed SOPs at every station, handwritten check sheets, and KPI and quality data that leaders only saw after the shift. The factory had no model line showing what a fully digital workshop should look like, and no dedicated space to train operators and engineers.

What was built

Line 601 became the model digital line: a live line dashboard, digital e-SOP tablets and ionizing (ESD) blowers at every station, and a leader workstation. TV walls above Workshop 6 stream KPI achievement, AOI inspection data and AI-CCTV of critical processes. Around it, an Engineering Training Center with four dojos and an ILTC room, plus a Wall of Fame, Product Showcase and Hall of Stars.

My part

Designed the full workshop layout and every station in 3D: workbench, leader's desk, display case and e-SOP frames. Planned and ran the schedule for all 15 sub-projects, managed vendors, including importing slide-rail material at half the local price, and supervised installation on site through to handover.

02

Factory Digital Twin & Changeover

A 3D replica of machines, workshops and the factory, used to run line changeovers

Sep 2025 – Oct 2026

Problem

Every layout decision was made on 2D AutoCAD drawings. Machines clashed with structures nobody had drawn, causing up to 15% delay during maintenance and machine moves, and expansion plans could not be checked before money was spent. Changeovers were tracked in two separate ME and BPM dashboards.

What was built

Machines measured on site and modeled in SolidWorks; Workshop 5, Workshop 6 and full-factory layouts in SketchUp and Enscape. On top: a 3D changeover dashboard and one merged ME–BPM changeover system with a shared task library and automatic planned-vs-actual timing.

My part

Measured and modeled 30+ machines myself and built the Workshop 5, Workshop 6 and full-factory models over 28 weeks. Produced the 3D line models the changeover dashboard runs on, and worked with the software team to merge ME and BPM changeovers into one system.

03

QA Pass Automation

Sensor-triggered IMEI scanning at the final packaging step

Dec 2025 – Oct 2026

Problem

The last check before boxes leave the line was fully manual. An operator confirmed the QA sticker, then scanned 10 IMEI codes one by one: 15–20 seconds per box, on every line, with nothing to stop a box with a missing sticker. The automated version existed on just one line.

What was built

An IR sensor detects the box, a color sensor confirms the white QA sticker, then an overhead camera reads all 10 IMEI codes at once. An andon lamp shows the result. A custom PCB and 3D-printed enclosure make every unit the same to install and maintain.

My part

Designed the sensor mount and enclosure; built, wired, programmed and tested the units with the team. Standardized the parts list and install steps, rolled them out line by line until all seven lines ran QA Pass, and closed with manuals and a formal handover to Manufacturing Engineering.

05

MPS v2.0 Production Planning

From the weekly master schedule to a daily line plan, plus IMEI risk tracking

Apr–Oct 2026

Problem

Planners rebuilt the daily line plan by hand in Excel, balancing hourly capacity, ramp-up curves, changeovers and line eligibility for dozens of phone models. One mistake meant lost output. IMEI stock risk was buried in a 12-sheet workbook with no early warning.

What was built

A planning engine that exports the plan in the factory's own Excel format; an on-premise AI assistant that answers questions from factory data; an IMEI risk dashboard ranking models by shortage.

My part

Set the accuracy target and adoption rules with the planners, divided the modules across the software team, and reviewed every release against real weekly schedules before it went live.

06

AI Vision for Quality

A camera as a second OK signal on packaging, and SOP compliance from CCTV

Apr–Oct 2026

Problem

On one packaging line a box only had to weigh right to pass, so an unsealed box could ship, and rejects left no evidence for QC. On assembly, supervisors could not watch every station for SOP compliance.

What was built

A seal-detection model running on an ordinary CPU; the PLC passes a box only when weigher and camera both say OK, and every NG is saved with a photo. VisionSOP brings several CCTV feeds into one dashboard that flags SOP violations.

My part

Defined the requirement with QC as a second signal rather than a replacement, split the hardware and software work, coordinated installation with ME, and reviewed model accuracy on real line data before release.
